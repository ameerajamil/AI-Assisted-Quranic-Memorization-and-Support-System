### Who is affected?

Children in primary school in need of assistance with memorising the Quran, especially those requiring assistance in sequence of verses and understanding the differences between verses of similar meaning. There are also relevant stakeholders such as Quran teachers who have the role of guiding children's memorisation and revising activities.

What is the problem?

Children may have a problem in recalling the order of the words of the Quraan and connecting the verses, particularly when they are unable to visualize the verses and to differentiate between similar words. While current digital Quran learning applications provide functionalities to identify the errors in the recitation, to track the memorization progress, to check the interaction of learners during learning, there is a need to investigate more integrated support through meaning-based visual clues and semantic relationships between verses.

Why is it important?

These challenges can impact children's recall of accuracy of memorization, as well as recall of similar verses. Repeated recitations may not be enough to give children further strategies to help them understand relationships between words and verses. Thus, it is necessary to investigate the possibility of using the concept of meaning in visual support in children's Quran memorization.

### Problem Statement

At the elementary level, the memorisation of Quranic verses can pose a problem for children, especially when they are asked to repeat what they hear, recall the exact order of words from the verse, and differentiate between the words and phrases of verses with similar meanings. The difficulties can occur if children have difficulties seeing the verses and finding links between the verses (Nastiti & Wahyuni, 2024). Present digital Quran learning applications have some functions like translation of errors when reading the Quran, tracking of progress, and interactive activities; however, more comprehensive support with meaning-based visual cues and semantic relationship among the verses in the Quran is needed (Isa et al., 2023). Thus, further research is needed on the potential of these approaches for supporting children's Quran memorization and recall.
